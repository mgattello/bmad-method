# PRD Quality Review: Todo App

Calibration: training capstone with explicit graded deliverables; single-user todo web app; chain-top (feeds architecture, stories, tests). Rigor applied: moderate. Done-ness and downstream usability weighted hardest because stories and tests will be derived from this document.

## Overall verdict

The PRD holds up. It has a real thesis ("a tiny, reliable, obviously-usable core is a better foundation than a broad one"), every FR carries testable consequences, the Glossary is used consistently, and the Non-Goals and Assumptions Index do genuine work. An Architect and a story author could start from it today.

What is at risk is narrower than the document's quality: four of the five Open Questions are product-owner confirmations the autonomous session could not obtain, and one of them (delete safety, OQ 2) would rewrite FR-9 and UJ-2 if answered the other way. The §6 acceptance table, which the PRD declares "fixed, not negotiable", silently drops graded deliverables that the training brief and the addendum both list. Two FR consequences (FR-11 concurrency, FR-14 retry) are ambiguous in ways a story author will have to guess at. None of these is structural; all are closable in a short confirmation pass.

## Decision-readiness: adequate

Decisions are stated as decisions, not considerations: optimistic update (FR-1), newest-first with no sort (FR-3), un-complete allowed (FR-8), immediate delete (FR-9), no edit in v1 (§8.2). The Vision names what was given up ("No accounts, no sharing, no priorities, no due dates") and §7 restates it as six concrete "Not a ..." lines. The Open Questions are genuinely open: OQ 1 gives three persistence options with the downstream consequence named ("Affects compose topology and FR-11 restart semantics"); OQ 2 lays out three delete-safety options with the real tension ("accidental taps on mobile are likely") rather than a rhetorical question.

What keeps this at adequate rather than strong: the PRD writes FR-9 as settled ("No confirmation step and no undo") while OQ 2 says the same thing is undecided and must be settled "before stories are written". Both are honest, but together they hand the story author a fork. The `[NOTE FOR PM]` device is used exactly once (§8.2, edit Description), at the softest of the tensions; the two sharper ones (delete safety, persistence technology) carry no inline callout at the FR they affect, and FR-11 does not point at OQ 1 at all.

### Findings
- **medium** FR-9 is decided and open at the same time (§4.4 FR-9, §10 OQ 2). The consequence "No confirmation step and no undo" will become a story and an E2E test; OQ 2 says the product owner may still pick "a short undo toast, or a confirm step". *Fix:* Make immediate delete the v1 decision with a one-line rationale (an undo toast is new UI surface and cuts against §1's "resists feature growth"), move undo/confirm to §8.2 as a v2 item, and reduce OQ 2 to a confirmation. If the owner genuinely may flip it, say so at FR-9 with `[NOTE FOR PM]` so the story author holds that story back.
- **low** Tension callouts are sparse (§4.5 FR-11, §10 OQ 1). OQ 1 names FR-11 as affected but FR-11 carries no pointer back, so a reader extracting FR-11 alone does not learn its restart semantics depend on an open architecture choice. *Fix:* Add "see Open Question 1" to FR-11's first consequence, mirroring the FR-9 to OQ 2 link.

## Substance over theater: strong

One persona (Dana), used in all three UJs, each of which drives a specific FR or edge case (UJ-1 edge case feeds FR-2; UJ-3 is the origin of FR-14 and FR-15). No differentiation section was written because none was needed; the PRD says plainly that the product "deliberately resists feature growth" and does not pretend the todo app is novel. The Vision could not swap into a generic todo PRD unchanged: it commits to specific observable behaviour ("reflected instantly, survives a refresh, and works the same on a phone as on a laptop") and names the dual purpose (training vehicle) rather than hiding it. NFRs carry product-specific thresholds almost everywhere (1 s / 200 Todos, 100 ms, 70%, WCAG 2.1 AA zero critical, 44 x 44 px, 360 px and 1280 px).

### Findings
- **low** "Builder's job" is not a user job (§2.1). The JTBD list ends with "demonstrate that a BMAD-driven, spec-first workflow can take a short PRD to a tested, containerised application." That is the exercise's job, already stated in §1 paragraph 3 and §0, and it blurs who the Target User section is about. *Fix:* Drop it from §2.1; §0 and §1 already carry it.

## Strategic coherence: strong

The thesis is stated and bet on: a minimal core, reliability over breadth, with the architecture left open to multi-user "without requiring a rewrite". Feature order follows it (capture, view, complete, delete, then persistence and error handling); nothing in §4 is there because it was easy. Success Metrics validate the thesis rather than activity: SM-1 (unguided completion) and SM-2 (zero data loss) map directly to the source PRD's two stated success conditions; SM-3 is the graded gate and is honestly labelled as such. The counter-metrics are real and sharp: SM-C1 makes feature growth "a regression against the thesis", and SM-C2 pre-empts coverage theater with a usable test ("whether tests would fail on a real regression"). MVP scope kind is experience-complete core, and §8.1/§8.2 match that.

### Findings
- **medium** SM-1's measurement will not happen as written (§9 SM-1). "Target: 5 of 5 test users, measured by observed usability session" is the primary metric, but a solo capstone has no usability panel and no section of the PRD, addendum, or brief schedules one. The thesis's headline metric would therefore go unmeasured and SM-3 would silently become the only primary metric. *Fix:* Either commit to the session (who, when, recorded where) or restate the target at the scale that will actually run: one unguided walkthrough by a peer, recorded in the QA report, plus the E2E suite as the regression proxy. Keep the source PRD's "without guidance" intent.

## Done-ness clarity: strong

This is where the PRD earns its keep. Fifteen FRs, each with a "Consequences (testable)" block, and nearly every bullet is a verifiable condition: "Submitting a valid Description adds exactly one Todo with Completion Status Active", "The API returns HTTP 400 with a machine-readable error body", "At 360 px wide and at 1280 px wide, all controls are reachable without horizontal scrolling and touch targets are at least 44 x 44 px", "the API never returns a 500 for user input". The Loading State consequence anticipates a real failure mode ("never shows the Empty State message, so a slow load is not mistaken for 'no tasks'"). FR-12 honestly says its verification is "by architecture review, not by an automated test" instead of faking a testable bullet. NFRs are bounded, not adjectival, with two exceptions below.

The findings are at the edges, but each is a point where a story author would have to guess.

### Findings
- **medium** FR-11 concurrency consequence is ambiguous and possibly untestable as written (§4.5 FR-11). "Concurrent requests from one client (for example rapid toggles) never leave a Todo in a state other than the last request's intended state." HTTP gives no ordering guarantee, so "last request" (last sent? last answered?) is undefined, and satisfying it needs a design choice the PRD does not make (client serialises writes per Todo, or server versioning). *Fix:* State the mechanism as the requirement: at most one in-flight write per Todo ID, later toggles coalesce to the final intended state; test as "two toggles within 50 ms end Active in the UI and on the API".
- **medium** Error State retry contract conflicts between Glossary and FR-14 (§3, §4.6 FR-14). Glossary: Error State is "what the app shows when an API call fails, with a way to retry." FR-14: a failed write "shows a non-blocking message naming the failed action" with no retry control mentioned; only the failed list load gets "a retry control". The graded E2E error-handling scenario depends on this. *Fix:* In FR-14 say explicitly that for create, toggle and delete the retry path is the original control (re-submit, re-tick) and no separate retry control is required; amend the Glossary to "with a way to retry (a retry control for list load; the original action otherwise)".
- **low** 100 ms optimistic bound is only testable with a slowed API (§4.1 FR-1, §9 SM-4). "visible ... within 100 ms of submission, before the API response arrives" cannot be asserted on a local stack where the API often answers in under 100 ms, and raw 100 ms timing assertions in browser E2E are flaky. *Fix:* Add to FR-1 and SM-4: "measured in E2E with the API delayed by at least 500 ms via route interception", and allow a looser wall-clock tolerance for the assertion itself.
- **low** Two NFR phrases are adjectives, not bounds (§5 Performance, Observability). "No noticeable jank on a mid-range phone" (neither term defined) and "Enough to debug a failing E2E test from logs alone". *Fix:* Replace jank with a Lighthouse mobile performance score threshold (the brief already names Lighthouse), or drop it; for Observability keep the method/path/status/duration list, which is already the bound, and drop the "enough to" sentence.
- **low** "Machine-readable error body" has no minimum shape (§4.1 FR-2, §4.5 FR-10). Integration tests for 400 and 404 need something to assert on beyond the status. *Fix:* Either name a minimum (JSON with a stable `code` and human `message`) or state that tests assert status plus presence of `code` and leave the full shape to architecture.

## Scope honesty: strong

§7 Non-Goals does real work: six explicit exclusions, each phrased as what the product is not, and including the non-obvious one ("Not an offline-first app ... it does not queue writes") that an optimistic-update design would otherwise invite. §8.2 names every deferred item with its reason and, where applicable, the Open Question that owns it. All eight distinct inferences are tagged inline and indexed (see Mechanical notes). De-scoping is proposed, not done silently: edit Description carries both an `[ASSUMPTION]` and a `[NOTE FOR PM]`. Open-items density (5 OQ, 8 assumptions, 1 note) is fine for the stakes; the material point is that four OQs are owned by the product owner and are confirmations rather than design work.

### Findings
- **medium** §6 drops graded deliverables while claiming to be the complete acceptance list (§6, addendum D). §6 says the criteria "are fixed, not negotiable by the team, and count as acceptance for the project as a whole", yet its "Process artefacts" row lists "PRD, architecture document, stories with acceptance criteria, QA reports" and omits the project brief, "Documentation of how BMAD guided the implementation", the Framework comparison, and the brief's "Phase 1-2 Deliverables" row. Addendum D lists all of these, and addendum A claims "this PRD is the output of" the brief step, so whether a separate project brief is owed is unresolved. *Fix:* Add the missing rows to §6 (or one row "all items in addendum D") and decide explicitly whether a project brief is a separate artefact or is satisfied by §0 to §2 of this PRD; record that decision in §6.
- **low** Unbounded list growth is implied, not stated (§4.2 FR-3, §4.5 FR-10, §5 Performance). With no edit, no archive and no "clear completed", the Todo List only grows; FR-10 says "list all Todos", the performance bound stops at 200. An Architect will ask whether the list endpoint pages. *Fix:* One `[NON-GOAL for MVP]` line: no pagination; the list endpoint returns every Todo; performance NFR is guaranteed to 200 Todos only.
- **low** Browser support is unstated (§5, §6). The E2E and accessibility targets are measured in a browser the PRD never names. *Fix:* One line in §5 or §6, for example current Chromium only, or the three Playwright engines.

## Downstream usability: strong

Glossary present and used with discipline across FRs, UJs and SMs: "Todo", "Todo List", "Description", "Completion Status", "Active"/"Completed", "Created At", "Persistence Store", "Empty/Loading/Error State", "Health Endpoint" appear capitalised and with one meaning. FR-1 to FR-15, UJ-1 to UJ-3, SM-1 to SM-5 and SM-C1/C2 are contiguous and unique; every "Realizes UJ-n" and "Validates FR-n" resolves. Each FR reads standalone; cross-references go through Glossary terms or explicit IDs, not "see above". UJs each carry Dana's context inline. The addendum keeps tooling out of the PRD body while giving the Architect what the brief prescribes. Minor drift and one broken section reference are in Mechanical notes; neither would mislead a downstream extraction.

## Shape fit: strong

Single-user consumer-style web app with meaningful UX, chain-top. The PRD takes the capability-first shape with three light UJs and one persona, which is the right weight: enough journey to anchor the error-handling and persistence requirements, not so much that UJs become overhead for a single-operator product. §6 as a graded-criteria table fits the training context. Persistence, health and extensibility are expressed as FRs (FR-10 to FR-13), which keeps them traceable to stories; FR-12 is really an architecture constraint, noted below.

### Findings
- **low** FR-12 is a constraint wearing an FR's clothes (§4.5 FR-12). Its consequence is explicitly "verified by architecture review, not by an automated test", so a story generated from it will have no runnable acceptance criterion. *Fix:* Keep the ID for traceability but label it "Architecture constraint" in the heading, or move it to §5 with the same wording, so the story author knows to produce an architecture-review checklist item rather than a test.

## Mechanical notes

**Glossary drift.** Reader-facing prose in §1 and §2.1 uses "task", "tasks" and "list" before the Glossary is introduced; acceptable there. Inside the UJs, which downstream workflows extract, two slips: UJ-2 "She then deletes an item" (should be Todo) and UJ-3 "shows an Error State saying the task could not be saved" (acceptable only if read as UI copy; make it "a Todo"). FR-5's "no tasks" is quoted UI copy and is fine. UJs say "ticks/unticks" where FRs say "completion control"/"toggle"; harmless but worth one Glossary line ("tick: user-facing word for activating the completion control"). §6 "single container-compose command" and §5 "container orchestrator" are deliberate vendor-neutral stand-ins for the addendum's "docker-compose"; the first is awkward phrasing.

**ID continuity.** FR-1 to FR-15 contiguous, no duplicates. UJ-1 to UJ-3, SM-1 to SM-5, SM-C1 and SM-C2 contiguous. All "Realizes" and "Validates" references resolve. OQ to FR links resolve (OQ 1 to FR-11, OQ 2 to FR-9 and §8.2, OQ 3 to FR-2, OQ 4 to FR-8, OQ 5 to §8.2). One broken section reference: §0 says assumptions are "indexed in §10", but §10 is Open Questions and the Assumptions Index is §11.

**Assumptions Index roundtrip.** Nine inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags, eight distinct (the 500-character cap appears at §3 and FR-2 and is indexed once as "§3, FR-2"). All eight distinct assumptions appear in §11; all eight §11 entries have an inline tag at the cited location. Roundtrip is clean. The workspace memlog records "all 6 assumptions"; the index has eight (the §2.2 single-store and FR-3 Created-At-not-displayed entries are the extra two). The memlog is not a deliverable; noting only so a later reconcile does not treat the index as over-full.

**UJ protagonist naming.** All three UJs name Dana with her context inline (freelance illustrator, phone then laptop, API outage). One persona is appropriate for a single-user product.

**Required sections.** Present for the stakes: Vision, Target User with JTBD and Non-Users, UJs, Glossary, Features/FRs, NFRs, Delivery Constraints, Non-Goals, MVP Scope, Success Metrics with counter-metrics, Open Questions, Assumptions Index. No inline `[NON-GOAL for MVP]` tags are used; §7 and the §4.4 "Out of Scope" sub-list carry the role.

**Addendum.** Section B states "Implies a Node/TypeScript stack for at least the frontend" as an untagged inference; it is an input to the Architect rather than a product requirement, but it would be cleaner tagged. Section D's note on the Framework comparison ("heading present in the brief, body not supplied; confirm expectations with course staff") is exactly the kind of honesty the PRD body should mirror in §6 (see the medium Scope honesty finding).
